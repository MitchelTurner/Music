A Sketch

A narrow town. A wide song.

Eli Roundy plays bluegrass and country from Ketchikan, Alaska — a narrow town on Revillagigedo Island, stacked between the Tongass and the tide. People call it the First City. They also call the rain liquid sunshine, which is optimistic, and not wrong.

The songs are string-band music with salt in the tuning pegs. Creek Street is a boardwalk on pilings; the water underneath keeps time better than a metronome. In summer the cruise ships fill the channel. In winter the porch is the venue. Deer Mountain stands behind town, and the fleet still goes out.

Ketchikan is the homeland of the Taant'a Kwáan Tlingit. This is a newcomer's porch music, played with respect for a shoreline that was already full of songs.

He plays guitar, banjo, and a fiddle that only behaves in damp weather. Wet Strings is the porch record: six tunes about weather, work, and the long way home along the Inside Passage. On a soaked afternoon the town has KRBD. Hit play here and the band tunes up in the browser. Bring a coat anyway.
